# Partner Organization Notes

Source: email from our partner contact, relaying responses from case workers at four shelters/houses. Each org answered three questions:

1. What specific locations are you mostly sending your clients to?
2. What transportation methods are your clients using?
3. What client barriers relate to transportation?

Some orgs returned more than one response (multiple case workers); these are merged below.

---

## Richmond House

**Common destinations**
- WorkBC-Ironwood (10–15 min walk from the house)
- Ironwood Public Library
- MSDPR office in Richmond
- Urgent & Primary Health Clinic on No. 3 Road
- Brighouse Drop-In Centre
- Caring Place (many non-profit agencies, near Richmond City Hall)
- VCH Transitions
- Anne Vogel Clinic
- ICBC, Lansdowne Mall
- Walk-in clinics and urgent care in general

Most frequent referrals: MSDPR, WorkBC, ICBC, walk-in clinics, urgent care, Anne Vogel Clinic.

**Transportation used**
- TransLink bus (sometimes given Compass tickets, if available)
- HandyDART
- Richmond Taxi
- TSA Kia van, case by case
- E-bikes and bikes
- A few guests own cars
- Guests usually ask staff for bus tickets to get to appointments

**Barriers**
- Low or no income
- Location: semi-industrial area, only one bus route with limited frequency, especially at night
- Guests walk 10–15 min from the highway to reach the stop
- Far from downtown Richmond; long bus times and transfers can be too much for some guests
- Mental and physical health conditions: guests often ask for rides and support
- Without staff support to get to appointments, they often don't go

## Cross-Org Takeaways

**Destinations**
- Health care dominates: community health centres (Three Bridges, Raven Song, Groundspring), hospitals (VGH, St. Paul's), and walk-in or urgent care clinics.
- Government and employment services are the second cluster: MSDPR, Service Canada, ministry offices, WorkBC, ICBC.
- Community and drop-in services come next: Kettle Society, Gathering Place, Recovery Cafe, Caring Place.
- Grace Mansion and Belkin House overlap heavily on Vancouver destinations (Three Bridges, Raven Song, Groundspring, VGH, St. Paul's). Richmond House is a separate, Richmond-local cluster.

**Transportation**
- Public transit is the default everywhere (bus, SkyTrain, HandyDART). Car ownership is rare.
- Ride share, taxi, and shelter vans are used occasionally or case by case.
- Shelters sometimes hand out bus tickets or Compass fares, and guests ask for them.

**Barriers**
- Cost is named by every org.
- Safety is named by Grace Mansion and Belkin House: clients avoid certain routes and stops.
- Location and transit coverage matter most for Richmond House: one infrequent bus, a long walk, and poor night service.
- Health conditions (mental and physical) make multi-transfer trips hard, and missed appointments follow when there's no staff escort.

## Implications for CareRide

- The core use case is validated: rides to medical, government, and employment appointments for low-income clients.
- Safety is a barrier separate from cost, so driver trust and vetting should be visible in the product.
- Richmond House shows demand for rides where transit exists but doesn't work: night trips, remote locations, and complex transfers.
- Ride requests may need to cover accessibility and companion support (HandyDART and TSA van usage, mental health needs). This relates to the existing wheelchair-access filtering.
- Frequent destinations could be offered as saved or suggested locations in the ride request form (the clinics, hospitals, and offices above).
- Richmond House says missed appointments are a direct consequence of lacking support. That suggests tracking appointment completion as an outcome metric.
- Both Richmond and Vancouver destinations show up, so driver coverage across Richmond and Vancouver matters.

## Open Questions

- Do clients need an escort or companion for the whole appointment, or just the ride? Richmond House implies the former for some guests.
- Are Compass-ticket and bus-ticket requests something CareRide should replace, or complement?
- What drives the "safety" concern: routes, time of day, or neighbourhood? This would shape how rides are matched to drivers.
