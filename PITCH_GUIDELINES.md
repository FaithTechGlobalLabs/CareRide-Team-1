# #HACKVAN2026 Project Update Guidelines

A Redemptive Technology Hackathon. Project Update Tips and Guidelines (6-8 minute presentations).

Project Updates are SHORT PRESENTATIONS describing the problem and your approach to addressing it (6-8 minutes). Your team presents what you all worked on at #HACKVAN and inspires the audience with perspectives or lessons learned. The pitch should answer three core questions:

1. WHAT IS IT?
2. HOW DOES IT WORK?
3. WHY DOES IT MATTER?

If you can communicate those three items clearly and quickly, then you can spend the remaining time demoing your solution. Resist the temptation to add more than this - trust us! If you have more, provide your audience a link so they can explore it themselves.

## Recommended Time Breakdown (6-8 mins total)

- **3 mins:** Story, Problem Definition, and Approach
- **2-3 mins:** The Result & Live Demo
- **2 mins:** Team Story, Lessons Learned, and Next Steps

## Pro Tips

### What makes a great pitch?

- TELL A STORY. Delivery is as important as content. Be authentic and connect with the room.
- MAKE YOUR POINTS and make them QUICKLY. The goal is not to tell everything, but to INSPIRE people to want to know more.

### What your audience is interested in

- To briefly hear about the solution you created and how it directly addressed the challenge, highlighting the areas where you INNOVATED or provided a BETTER UNDERSTANDING of the possibilities (Slides 2, 3, 4, & 5).
- To give you a brief chance to share the team's inspiring STORIES, CHALLENGES, and SUCCESSES (Slides 6, 7, & 8).
- To give others on your team and in the room the CONTEXT that can't be read online or viewed in your GitHub repo (Slides 2 & 3).

## Judging Criteria

1. **Problem.** E.g. How well does the team understand the problem? How much additional research has gone into existing solutions?
2. **Potential.** E.g. How many people does this problem (and thus, project) affect? What's the potential for the project to spread to other organizations and spheres?
3. **Approach.** E.g. Does the proposed solution adequately address the problem? Is the solution feasible? Is the solution efficient and effective?
4. **Deliverable.** E.g. If an MVP, did the MVP fulfill the core functionality during the hackathon? If a prototype, is it adequately prepared to test the core assumptions of the project?
5. **Excellence.** E.g. How well did the project team do given their potential (e.g. professional experience, skillset etc.) and constraints (e.g. time frame, project owner's requirements, unexpected obstacles, team assigned, etc.)?
6. **Product Plan.** E.g. How well will it scale beyond the MVP? How much consideration has been given to integration with the client’s technical stack?

### Scoring

- 1-5 scale, relative across teams
- 1 = not meeting criteria, 5 = very good
- Scores are relative, not absolute (judges compare teams against each other)

## Slideshow Guidelines

Please create a slideshow with 7-8 slides:

1. **Project Title Page:** Name of project, 1-sentence summary of the problem, link to Git repository, and names + photos of team members.
2. **Problem Definition (#1):** Characteristics of the problem presented to us on pitch night (Friday).
3. **Potential Impact:** What would be possible (or impossible) if we addressed these problems?
4. **Our Approach:** Were you building an MVP or prototype? And what angle did the team take, and what were some of your assumptions?
5. **The Result & Demo:** Short summary of design, product, and any other deliverable. (This is the ideal time to run your Live Demo!)
6. **The Story:** Who did what, and what were some successes and challenges along the way?
   - **Spotlight (Optional):** Celebrate a teammate who particularly excelled this weekend; someone whose talent, character, (or both) is typically unrecognized.
7. **Thank you & Next Steps:** How can the audience support the project's future?
